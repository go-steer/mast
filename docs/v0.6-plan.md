# mast v0.6 plan — enforcement that reaches, and a ceiling that stops a call

**Date:** 2026-08-30 · **Status:** plan, not a design decision · **Target:** close the two places where mast's enforcement is weaker than the bundle says it is — a mutating call inside a planner dispatch that no gate sees, and a budget ceiling that is crossed by the call that reports it

This is the continuation of [`./v0.5-plan.md`](./v0.5-plan.md), which continues [`./v0.4-plan.md`](./v0.4-plan.md) and [`./v0.3-plan.md`](./v0.3-plan.md). That first document's §2 test tiers and §3 workstream IDs are still canonical; this plan opens **W9** and **W10**, the first IDs since W8.

Like its predecessors this is a planning artifact: decisions it surfaces belong in the owning design doc and [`./README.md`](./README.md)'s resolved-decisions table. No docs-site mirror — the roadmap page carries the user-visible half.

---

## 0. The scoreboard no longer supplies a spine

v0.3, v0.4 and v0.5 were all organized by the same thing: the parity scoreboard in [`./v0.3-plan.md`](./v0.3-plan.md) §1. Pick the red rows, flip them, ship. That spine is spent. The board reads **17 of 19** at the v0.5.0 tag, and the two rows still red — in-chat Approve/Reject and the approver allowlist — are [switchboard's](https://github.com/go-steer/switchboard/issues/40) [to write](https://github.com/go-steer/switchboard/issues/65). mast's side of them shipped in #248 as a wire-contract test.

**v0.6 is the first release since the fork that has to state its own claim.** The board will not choose for us, and "whatever is left in `Further out`" is a backlog, not a release. So this plan starts by naming the claim and defending it, rather than by listing rows.

The scoreboard stays where it is and stays canonical for parity. It simply stops being the agenda.

## 1. The claim

> **No mutating call goes unrecorded, no call is paid for before it is checked, and where enforcement cannot reach, mast refuses rather than pretends.**

*(That third clause is a late amendment. The claim opened as "enforcement reaches every call the workload makes" — §2 records why that is not achievable in one of mast's own shapes, and a release claim that survives its own investigation unchanged usually means the investigation was decorative.)*

Two gaps sit behind the sentence, and both have the same shape from an operator's chair: *the bundle says something that the runtime does not fully deliver.*

**(a) A mutating call inside a planner dispatch is recorded nowhere and gated by nothing.** `invoke_specialist` constructs its runner in the tool body with no plugins, so neither the effect outbox nor the write gate reaches a call the dispatched specialist makes. Measured, not inferred — an outer `BeforeToolCallback` over that shape sees `invoke_specialist` and `finish_task` and never the `scale_deployment` executed between them ([#235](https://github.com/go-steer/mast/issues/235)). Contained since #237 by refusing the combination at composition, which closes the silent-violation path and does not close the hole. The two halves come apart under measurement (§2): the **recording** half is closable and W9.3 closes it; the **gate** half is not, and v0.6's job there is to stop calling a permanent constraint a temporary containment.

**(b) A ceiling is crossed by the call that reports it.** `budget.Meter.Observe` folds usage out of the event stream after a call returns, so the first thing that happens when a workload runs out of money is that it spends more money. And a crossed *specialist* ceiling stops the session rather than handing the coordinator a refusal it could route around, which turns a per-specialist cap into a session-killer.

Neither is a bug in the sense that something reports the wrong answer. Both are cases where a bundle's `hitl.on_mutation` or `budget.max_usd` promises an operator more than the runtime enforces — which is the specific failure this product exists to not have.

### What this release is not

Not parity. Not a new surface. Nothing here adds a tool, a bundle block a user must learn, or an endpoint. If v0.6 ships correctly, an existing bundle behaves the way its author already believed it did — with one exception that is now the point rather than an embarrassment: the composition refusal on a planner roster holding a change executor **stays**, and starts saying why instead of apologizing.

## 2. The seam inventory, and the premise this plan had to abandon

The [roadmap](https://go-steer.github.io/mast/roadmap/) says of pre-call budget gating: *"Both need a seam in front of the model call rather than behind it."* It is easy to read "both" as *#235 and budget*, and to conclude the two are one piece of work. **They are not, and this plan was drafted on that wrong premise before it was traced.** The "both" in that sentence is budget's own two problems. Recording the correction here so it is not re-derived:

| Concern | Mechanism today | File | Crosses a planner dispatch? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Effect outbox | runner plugin, `BeforeToolCallback` | `pkg/effects/effects.go:274` | **No** |
| Write gate | runner plugin, `BeforeToolCallback` | `pkg/approval/plugin.go:141` | **No** |
| Spend metering | folds `session.Event`s in the **caller's event loop** | `pkg/budget/budget.go:226`, installed at `mast.go:636`, `cmd/mast/main.go:2755`, `cmd/mast/subrun.go:173` | **Yes**, via `SubRunSink` |
| Watchdog | same out-of-band channel | `cmd/mast/subrun.go:192` | **Yes** |

They are different mechanisms in different layers. The plugins are runner-scoped and the sub-runner at `pkg/planner/dispatch.go:284` is constructed with no `PluginConfig`. Metering is not a plugin at all, and is post-hoc *by construction* — `Observe` can only see usage that a returned event already reports.

So **v0.6 is two workstreams that ship under one claim, not one piece of work.** Saying so up front is cheaper than discovering it in the third PR.

### The transfer that is real

`pkg/planner/dispatch.go:256` states the useful fact in its own words:

> *"The inner runner's accounting reaches the host through `obs` (#226). Its PLUGINS do not, and that is the larger hole on the same seam."*

That is standing proof that **a host-owned concern can cross the dispatch boundary without being a plugin.**

### What the probes settled

Three shapes were on the table for #235 — a durable sub-session under a derived id, gating the dispatch boundary itself, or keeping the refusal — plus a fourth this plan proposed: carry the decision out-of-band the way spend does. Measurement decided among them (2026-08-31, PR [#255](https://github.com/go-steer/mast/pull/255)); the decision is recorded in [`./README.md`](./README.md)'s resolved-decisions table.

**The write gate cannot cross, for a structural reason.** A park is two halves and both live in the session event log: `RequestConfirmation` writes the question there (`pkg/approval/plugin.go:239`) and the callback then returns normally with a "parked, stop" result — the turn is *not* suspended. Resume matches against that log and re-enters at the **root** (`pkg/approval/dispatchseam_test.go`, two green probes). A dispatch sub-session is in-memory and dies with the tool call, and nothing re-enters a dispatch mid-flight. Coordinator and graph dispatch get the gate at full per-call fidelity precisely *because* they share the log. That is the trade: **planner dispatch exists to keep a private session, and a private session is exactly what puts a resume round trip out of reach.**

Gating the boundary instead was the tempting escape, and it is disqualified on its own terms. `invokeSpecialistArgs` is two fields — a specialist name and a prose string — so the operator would approve an *intention*: no typed arguments to review, nothing to edit (`pkg/approval/edit.go`), no `per_change_set` grant to bind, N mutations collapsing into one park, and a `Decision` record naming `invoke_specialist` rather than the change. Lead row **L7** distinguishes mast from *"a static list of tool names to interrupt on; the arguments are whatever the model produces on resume"* ([`./v0.3-plan.md`](./v0.3-plan.md):61). Boundary gating **is** that right-hand column.

**The outbox is a different problem with a different answer.** Recording is one-directional, so the resume objection does not apply. Two probes measured its preconditions:

- *Is a sink-observed intent actually an intent?* **Yes.** `pkg/planner/outboxseam_test.go` measures `[sink_saw_call, tool_body_ran, sink_saw_response]` — the sink is handed the mutating `FunctionCall` **before** the tool body runs, so intent-before-effect survives the boundary. Asserted rather than logged: ADK owns that ordering, and a bump reversing it would silently invalidate the design.
- *What would a durable sub-session buy?* **Nothing.** `pkg/transcript/dispatchscope_test.go` shows `Store.ScanInterrupted` lists sessions for one `AppName` while the sub-runner uses `"planner_dispatch"` — so a durable sub-session is invisible to the boot pass that exists to act on its dangling intents. Durability was never the binding constraint; **reachability by the workload's own scan** is.

Installing the outbox plugin on the sub-runner would also buy nothing: `pkg/effects` keeps no store of its own, deriving intents and completions from the session log through `beforeRun` → `scanHistory` → `pairScan`.

So the record has to land where the workload's scan already looks — the outer session — and the one open question is *where in it*, without polluting the planner's model context. Ordinary events would put the specialist's individual tool calls in front of the planner's model and defeat the isolation the whole shape exists for. The precedent is `approval.EditStateKeyPrefix`: a durable record in session **state**, which the model never sees. `ScanDangling` reads events only, so it needs teaching about a state-side record.

### The third seam, for W10

A `model.LLM` wrapper travels with the model object rather than with the runner, so it reaches a dispatched specialist for free. The shape is already proven in this codebase — `internal/evals/judge/retry.go:107` wraps `adkmodel.LLM` to retry a provider's 429/503 — but only in evals. The production construction point is `internal/compose.BuildModel` (`internal/compose/compose.go:507`). That is the natural home for a check that must run *before* a call is issued, and W10.1 should weigh it against an `llmagent` `BeforeModelCallback`.

**W10.1 weighed it and the callback won** — see the task entry below. The paragraph above is left as written because its error is the instructive part: "reaches a dispatched specialist for free" is true, and it is also true of the callback, so it was never the discriminator. A runner plugin is dropped at the dispatch boundary; an agent callback is part of the agent object the dispatcher wraps.

## 3. Tasks

Six, one PR each — five planned plus W10.0, which the release added once it was clear W10.2 turns a price into an input to a refusal. Two chains, and they do not block each other — W9 and W10 can run in parallel.

### W9 — enforcement reaches a dispatched call, as far as it can

W9.1 was a decision task and is **done** (2026-08-31, PR [#255](https://github.com/go-steer/mast/pull/255) for the measurements, §2 above for the argument); W9.3 and W9.2 both shipped 2026-09-01. **The W9 chain is complete and [#235](https://github.com/go-steer/mast/issues/235) is closed.** It came in smaller than the plan first assumed because the gate half turned out to be a constraint rather than a bug — the work there was making five artifacts stop calling it a stopgap, and proving the escape they point at actually builds.

- [x] **W9.1 — decide the boundary** *(design)*. Settled: **the write gate stays out of a planner dispatch, permanently, and the refusal becomes the design rather than containment.** The outbox half is separable and does not inherit the obstacle. Recorded in [`./README.md`](./README.md)'s resolved-decisions table.

- [x] **W9.2 — make the refusal permanent, and say why in the artifact** *(2026-09-01; no runtime change)*. Five artifacts described `CheckPlannerWriteSurface` as containment that comes out with whatever settles [#235](https://github.com/go-steer/mast/issues/235). That promise is now the opposite of true, and a reader who believed it would wait for something that is not coming. The check's own doc comment and `reference/write-gate.md` were already corrected by W9.1 and W9.3; this closes `DESIGN.md` (whose contract bullet still ended "Still owed" for a record that had shipped), `concepts/specialists-and-dispatch.md`, `roadmap.md` (which also carried a **Next** bullet offering the two rejected boundary designs as the plan), and `sibling-sync.md` — the last with a verified rather than assumed no-port verdict: core-agent has no `invoke_specialist` and no dispatch tool that builds a runner in a tool body, so the seam does not exist there. **The exit criterion was the composition test, and it earned the distinction.** The refusal names three ways forward — `coordinator`, `graph`, `on_mutation: apply` — and `TestEveryEscapeTheRefusalNamesActuallyComposes` builds each through both doors (`CheckRoster` and `BuildRoot`), while `TestTheRefusalNamesEveryWayOut` pins the words and fails on hedging language. Both were shown to fail under mutation: dropping `graph` from the message reddens the first, and a graph roster missing its classifier reddens the second. A stopgap can name a way out loosely; an answer cannot, because an escape nobody composed costs the operator the hour they spend on it. #235 is closed.

- [x] **W9.3 — the outbox record, out-of-band, under `apply`** *(the only new mechanism in W9)*. **Done.** The refusal exempts `on_mutation: apply` because there was no gate to bypass, but the missing outbox record was real there and cost exactly-once replay after an interrupted dispatch. This is the leg an unattended monitor actually needs, since `apply` is what an unattended workload sets when nobody is there to approve.

  Shipped shape: a host-side recorder on the #226 observer seam (`effects.SubRunRecorder`) writes each dispatch's mutating intents and completions to the outer session's **companion ops row** — *not* primary-row session state as this plan first proposed; see the answered open question in §4 for why that was impossible. `effects.Config.ExternalDangling` folds them in at `beforeRun`, before the ack filter, and `DanglingScan.WithExternal` folds them into the auto-resume scan. The recorder runs **last** on the seam, after the budget meter and the watchdog, because an intent record claims a call is *about* to happen and every consumer ahead of it can still stop the sub-run; recording first would wedge a session into ambiguous-effect mode over a mutation a ceiling prevented. A failed intent write stops the dispatch — under `apply` the record is the only control the call has.

  Tests: `internal/compose/subrunrecord_test.go` kills a dispatch mid-mutation and asserts the dangling intent is scannable while the log's own scan sees nothing, plus the negative assertion that the outer log carries no `scale_up` call; `pkg/effects/subrun_test.go` pins the refusal and the ack that lifts it; `pkg/transcript/subrun_test.go` pins the round trip over both backends and that an out-of-band record does not invalidate a live write lease.

### W10 — A ceiling stops a call before it is paid for

- [x] **W10.0 — price the (backend, model) pair** *(2026-09-01; unplanned, added because W10.2 changes what a price is for)*. Through v0.5 a price was something mast *reported*: a number on a meter, next to a ledger row, in a release note. W10.2 makes it something mast **refuses with** — the input to a decision that stops a call from being issued. A number that is merely reported can be approximately right. A number a refusal is computed from is either the price of the call about to be made or it is not.

  It was not. `RatePer1K` took a model id and nothing else, and `pkg/pricing`'s builtin table is keyed on bare model ids that are a **mixture of two backends**: LiteLLM's unprefixed `claude-*` rows are first-party Anthropic, its unprefixed `gemini-*` rows are Vertex. Every Claude-on-Vertex call in mast's history was priced off the first-party row and every Gemini-on-the-Developer-API call off the Vertex row, by construction, with nothing in the type saying so.

  The fix is the pair, not a patch: rates are now keyed on `<backend>/<model>` and the price is looked up for the pair the call will actually be billed against. Four backends — `anthropic`, `anthropic-vertex`, `gemini`, `vertex` — matching the names the client builders already use. `dev/regen-builtin-pricing` now emits a second table, `builtinByBackend`, alongside the bare one; the generator used to discard every upstream key containing a `/`, which is exactly the set of rows that says what a backend charges. 58 qualified rows for 31 models.

  **The alias is not the backend**, and getting that wrong would have moved the defect rather than fixed it. `--provider` is an alias; the backend is resolved from the alias *and* the environment — `GOOGLE_GENAI_USE_VERTEXAI` sends a bare `--provider` to Vertex, and Anthropic picks first-party or Vertex by which credential is present. So the resolver is **shared with the code that builds the client**: `compose.Backend` and `compose.anthropicBackend` are the same functions `BuildModel` uses, so a price cannot name a backend the call did not use. `pricing.LookupFor(backend, model)` tries the qualified key and falls back to the bare id, so a pair upstream does not price keeps the rate it has today rather than dropping to zero.

  **This moves no number.** Measured against LiteLLM before building: all 12 shipped Claude models agree exactly between `anthropic` and `vertex_ai-anthropic_models` on input, output, cache-read, cache-write and window, and every shipped Gemini model agrees between `gemini` and `vertex_ai-language-models`. That agreement is upstream's to keep and not mast's to depend on — which is the argument for keying on the pair, not against it. It also means no test built on the shipped table can tell a pair-keyed lookup from a bare one, so the discriminating test (`TestRatePer1K_FollowsTheResolvedBackend`) prices the pairs apart in a synthetic catalog and drives the six env-and-alias combinations through the real resolver.

  Closes the live defect [#178](https://github.com/go-steer/mast/issues/178) left behind (see §6) and delivers the pricing half of model-support M2.

- [x] **W10.1 — pick the pre-call seam** *(2026-09-02; design + spike, no runtime change)*. Settled: **the seam is an `llmagent` `BeforeModelCallback`, and a refusal is a synthesized response rather than an error or a cancel.** `pkg/planner/precallseam_test.go` measures both candidates on the one shape that discriminates them — a planner dispatch — and the plan's own reasoning above (§2, "the third seam") turns out to be true of the wrong axis.

  **Reachability does not separate them; both reach.** The callback fires inside a dispatch, and for the reason the plan expected to rule it out: it is agent-scoped, and `newDispatcher` wraps the specialist *agent* that was built at startup, callbacks and all. Runner plugins are what the boundary drops — that was always the whole of #235 — and a callback is not one.

  What separates them is **whose promise the identity is**. A per-agent ceiling needs the agent name, because `budget.Meter` keys scopes on it. The callback is handed `agent.Context` as a declared parameter. The wrapper can get there too — ADK passes its `agent.InvocationContext` as `GenerateContent`'s `ctx`, so a type assertion recovers the name — but `model.LLM` declares `context.Context`, so that is an undocumented property of a dependency, and an ADK bump could pass a plain context without changing a single signature. The spike pins the coincidence in a test so it would die loudly rather than fail open. Granularity says the same thing twice: `compose` caches models by name, so **one wrapper object serves N agents** while a callback is per-agent by construction.

  The wrapper's real advantage is coverage it cannot forget, and the callback's one weakness is the mirror of it — a construction site that omits it fails **open**. That is a finite, enumerable wiring problem and W10.2 owns closing it, which is a better trade than depending on a property nobody promised. *(This entry first put the count at seven across three files. It is eleven — `mast.go`, `internal/compose/oneshot.go` ×2, `cmd/mast/main.go`, `pkg/planner/planner.go`, `pkg/specialists/register.go` ×2, `pkg/router/router.go`, `internal/toolcatalog/catalog.go` ×3, before the examples and evals. The correction is not a footnote: "seven places that must each remember" is a wiring task, and eleven-and-growing is a defect waiting for the twelfth, which is what pushed W10.2 to install the callback inside the three `pkg/agent` constructors instead of at the sites.)*

  **A refusal is a synthesized response.** Both shapes genuinely skip the provider, so both are pre-call; they differ in what the caller is handed. Returned as an error, the refusal reaches the planner as `{"error": "invoke_specialist \"sp\": workflow: dynamic child …: budget: refused before the call"}` — the reason survives, but in the field ADK uses for a broken tool and wrapped in three layers of workflow plumbing. `dispatch.go` already refused to emit that shape for a crossed cap ("*a cap that fires must not look like a broken tool*"), and a pre-call ceiling one layer down should not contradict it. Synthesized, it arrives as an ordinary report carrying `sub_model_calls: 0` — the pre-call property visible in the planner's own view of the dispatch. The mechanism is not new either: `FinishOnStall` already converts a Task agent's silent turn into a `finish_task` call so it costs its own part of the answer instead of the run.

  **One finding constrains W10.2 whichever seam had won.** Inside a dispatch, *neither* seam sees the outer session — both see the sub-runner's `invoke-<name>`. Since mast pools one meter per session id, a check resolving its meter from `ctx.SessionID()` there would mint a fresh empty meter and approve every call, enforcing a ceiling against a budget that restarts at zero. Same problem #226 solved for accounting and W9.3 for recording, and this entry proposed the same answer: bind at the dispatch door through the existing `SubRunObserver`, where the tool context still belongs to the outer turn. *(W10.2 found a better one and did not need it — see below.)* Also recorded: **no refusal on this path ends the operator's run.** The session stop W10.3 is about lives in the post-hoc fold on the *coordinator* path, which calls `cancel()`.

- [x] **W10.2 — refuse the call that would cross the ceiling** *(2026-09-02)*. The post-hoc fold is untouched: `Observe` is still the ledger, still durable, and still the thing #175 persists. In front of it now sits `budget.Meter.Allow`, asked by `agent.RefuseOnGate` before every model call.

  **`Allow` is not `Observe` run early.** `Observe` asks "has a ceiling been crossed"; `Allow` asks "can this ceiling still be respected", and the second is answerable without guessing what the next call will cost. It never estimates the call's size — a pre-call check built on a projection refuses affordable work on a bad guess and permits unaffordable work on a worse one, and an operator cannot tell those apart. It refuses only where the arithmetic is a proof: `calls+1 > MaxTurns` is exact, and `tokens >= MaxTokens` / `cost >= MaxCostUSD` hold because every real call adds something. That `>=` against `crossed`'s `>` is the whole gap, and the gap is exactly one call — at cost *equal* to the cap the fold permits another and then reports the overshoot.

  On turns the fold is not merely late, it is structurally unable to be right: `MaxTurns` is checked with `>`, so **a workload capped at 3 turns has always made 4**. That is the acceptance test, and it runs as a two-column table where the ungated column *is* v0.5's behaviour, so the diff between before and after is an assertion rather than a claim: at 3 of 3, ungated makes a fourth call, prices it, writes it to the ledger and *then* aborts; gated makes zero, and the meter and the ledger both still read three.

  **A refusal is not a cancel, and it leaves no phantom spend.** The refusal is in the transcript where an operator reads it (`agent.RefusalMarker`, testable through `agent.Refused`), and the ledger's row count and the meter's cost are asserted equal in both columns, because a refusal that wrote to one and not the other reconciles wrong — worse than either being late.

  **What a refusal costs nothing *also* means is that nothing above it runs out.** Two things fell out of that, and both were found by running the change rather than reasoning about it. First, the turn driver reported **OK**: the stream ends cleanly, the session holds an answer, and a caller's `if err != nil` never fired for work that did not happen. `Meter.Refusals()` — a count and the first reason, snapshotted before the turn and diffed after — is what turns the clean ending back into an error, and it is a *delta* rather than a "is a ceiling precluding now?" question on purpose: a run that finished its work and happened to land exactly on its cap did not stop for the budget. Second, and worse: the UAT hung. A retry loop above the model — the write gate's change-set contract, which hands a specialist its refused report back and gives it another turn — was bounded through v0.5 by the ceiling it was burning. Made free, it is bounded by nothing; the leg that used to fail loudly at 5 calls spun to 3,130. So the refusal check runs **inside** the event loop and cancels, the same place and the same way the post-hoc fold does, with the post-loop check kept as the backstop for a refusal that surfaces no event on the outer stream. The general form is worth keeping: *making an error cheap removes whatever the expense was silently load-bearing for.*

  **The preflight door had the mirror of that bug**, opened and closed in the same change. It asked `Trips()` — has anything *crossed* — but a well-behaved session now stops **on** its cap without crossing, so the door swung open and the session would answer "I am out of budget" in prose forever. It asks `PrecludedAll()` now. *(W10.3 narrowed that to `PrecludedSession()`: once a spent specialist no longer ends the run, a door keyed on the whole meter would refuse turns a half-spent roster can still serve.)*

  **The operator surface reports one event with two codes.** `ClassifyTurnError` prefix-matches `budget exceeded`; `ErrRefused` reads `budget refused the call` and was falling through to `unknown` — no kind, no hint, no reset endpoint, which is exactly the regression #208 fixed. Both are `cost_ceiling` and both carry the reset hint, because from outside this is one wall; they differ only in `code` (`BUDGET_REFUSED` / `BUDGET_EXCEEDED`), because only one of them spent money.

  **The gate rides the Go context, not the agent.** Agents are built once per daemon; a ceiling belongs to a session. This also disposes of W10.1's constraint above without needing the dispatch-door binding it proposed: context values *do* survive the boundary (`dispatch.go` derives its sub-context with `WithAgentCancel`, a shallow copy), so a gate installed on the turn's context reaches a planner-dispatched specialist as it stands. That is strictly better than resolving a meter from `ctx.SessionID()`, which would have missed and failed open. `WithCallGate` goes in at the two metered run sites — `cmd/mast/main.go`'s `runTurnPre` and `mast.go`'s `runTurn`. One-shot mode has no meter to install, and an absent gate permits: "unmetered" and "metered with no ceilings" are different statements and neither one stops a workload.

  **The eleven construction sites are closed by construction, not by wiring.** `gated()` installs `RefuseOnGate` inside `NewTaskAgent`, `NewSingleTurnAgent` and `NewCoordinator`, first in the chain so a caller's own callback cannot short-circuit past the ceiling. That recovers the un-forgettability the wrapper had without taking on its dependence on undocumented ADK behaviour — the one real cost of the seam W10.1 chose, paid off rather than merely noted.

- [x] **W10.3 — a crossed specialist ceiling is a refusal, not a session stop** *(2026-09-02)*. The coordinator keeps the session and gets back a report it can route around, the same as a specialist that declines. Through v0.5 the opposite held, and it was never a decision — cancelling the run was the only lever the fold had, so "one path is spent" and "the workload is over" came out the same way because nothing could tell them apart.

  **The thing that tells them apart is typed.** `budget.Scope(err) (string, bool)` answers *whose* ceiling an enforcement error was; `scopedTo` wraps at the two places that raise one (`Observe`'s specialist branch, `Allow`'s refusal) and changes neither the sentinel nor a byte of the message, because `pkg/attach.ClassifyTurnError` prefix-matches that text on purpose (#135/#208) and must keep working without importing `pkg/budget`. It is an error type rather than a substring test on the specialist's name for the reason W10.1 gave about the ADK context: a difference in outcome should not rest on prose. `pkg/budget/scope_test.go` pins the sentinel, the exact text and the prefix.

  **Report every refusal; stop for only the workload's own.** That splits one counter into two — `Refusals()` is the reporting count, `SessionRefusals()` the stopping one, both still read as a delta across the turn per W10.2. Keeping the *first reason* separately in each matters more than the counts: the first refusal of a turn is usually a specialist's, and a driver that stopped on the session's cap while quoting a specialist's reason would send an operator to raise the wrong ceiling. The post-hoc fold gets the mirror branch — a scoped `ErrExceeded` logs `BUDGET CEILING — a specialist crossed its own cap; the run continues` and the loop goes on; an unscoped one still cancels.

  **The runtime change is four branches; the work is what it does to reporting.** A run that quietly loses half its roster now returns the same `nil` as one that did not, which is the failure mode W10.2's "OK for work that never happened" already warned about, one level up. Four surfaces answer it, each pinned by a test shown to fail without it: the trip metric (`mast_budget_trips_total`) and a WARN line naming the specialist, `cost_ceiling.scopes[]` on `GET /guardrails`, and `mast.Result.Exhausted` for a library caller — scoped rows only, since the workload's own ceiling is already in the error that caller is holding instead of a `Result`.

  **The guardrail surface had to be made symmetric, and was not.** Two bugs, both from the meter's answers now spanning a roster while the endpoint asks about one holder. `reset()` computed `costTrips` from the whole meter but `remaining` from the scope, so a **session** grant on a workload with a spent specialist reported `Reset: [cost_ceiling]` — claiming to have cleared a trip it never touched. Its mirror appeared the moment that was fixed: the same grant then reported a cheerful "nothing was tripped" while a specialist sat spent, the exact foot-gun W10.3 removed from the session path reappearing on the operator's. `tripsForScope` keeps the two apart everywhere (a session reset is not judged by a specialist's ceiling, nor a specialist's by the session's), and `exhaustedNote` adds the tail that names who is still out and the `scope=` to raise them with — names rather than a count, because "1 specialist" sends the operator back to `GET /guardrails` to find out which.

  **Making a refusal cheap made retrying one free.** W10.2's sharpest finding recurred a level up, and the e2e caught it: cancelling the session on any refusal had been masking a loop. A Task agent that declares an `output_schema` has a finish_task whose *parameters are that schema*, so `DefaultRefusalPayload`'s `{"result": …}` does not validate — and ADK answers an invalid finish_task with "you could retry calling this tool", a retry instruction and not an error. Refused call, invalid report, retry, refused call, and no iteration costs a model call. Not hypothetical: the v0.4 UAT's producer-contract legs drive a specialist into its own `max_turns`, and they spun to **3,292 finish_task calls in ninety seconds**, passing under v0.5 only because the session died first. The fix declines to guess — `UnreportableRefusal` returns no arguments, `RefuseOnGate` reads that as "do not report at all" and answers in plain text, and the delegation resolves to nothing. Fabricating a conforming finding was available (`conformingArgs` exists) and is the one thing this must not do: a refusal is exactly the case where nothing was looked at, so a synthesized one would put an invented fault into an incident stream. An unresolved delegation is a worse answer than a good report and a much better one than a spin. `pkg/agent/refusalreport_test.go` bounds the run and treats the bound as the assertion, because before the fix the test does not fail, it hangs. The two UAT legs changed contract with it: a specialist's spent cap is no longer a 500, so they assert a ceiling on the retries and that the closed path is on the record at WARN — the loudness moved off the status code, and had to be shown to still exist.

  **The acceptance test is a two-arm table with the uncapped arm as the counterfactual**, and it lives in `cmd/mast/budget_roster_test.go` rather than `pkg/agent` because the half worth proving is the driver's, not the gate's. A scripted coordinator delegates `alpha, alpha, beta`; uncapped it makes both alpha calls, and with `alpha` capped at one turn it makes one, is refused, and finishes the workload through beta. Asserted together: the turn returns `nil`, `Refusals()` is 1 and `SessionRefusals()` 0, `Precluded("alpha")` is non-empty while `Precluded("beta")` is empty, and the transcript carries the refusal marker, `alpha`, `cap of 1` **and** `handled by beta` — the loss and the route-around in the same log an operator reads.

**Board impact: none.** No parity row moves — all five of these are behind rows that are already green, which is exactly why the board could not have chosen this release.

## 4. Decisions this release has to make

- ~~**What a park inside a dispatch resumes into**~~ **(answered, W9.1).** Nothing. A resume re-enters at the root and matches the parked call against a session log the sub-run does not share; no mechanism re-enters a dispatch mid-flight. That is what makes the gate's absence structural rather than unwired.
- ~~**Whether one answer authorizing a whole specialist is an approval or a blank cheque**~~ **(answered, W9.1).** A blank cheque, and disqualifying: it is L7's own description of the competitor. Boundary gating is off the table.
- ~~**Where in the outer session a sub-run's intent record lives**~~ **(answered, W9.3).** The **companion ops row** (`<sessionID>:mast-ops`), not primary-row session state. The `EditStateKeyPrefix` precedent this plan proposed does not transfer: `AppliedEdit` is written *in-band*, as an `Actions.StateDelta` on an event the runner is already appending. A dispatch's record is out-of-band by construction, and ADK's database session service enforces optimistic concurrency — a session handle is a write lease, and an out-of-band append to the primary row invalidates every other holder's handle. A dispatch writes at precisely the moment the outer turn holds one, so primary-row state would have killed the very turn it was recording. That is the constraint `opsSuffix` already exists for. The ops row is also skipped by `List`, `ScanInterrupted` and `findUserID`, and read on every `Get`, so the record is reachable without being a session of its own; `pkg/transcript/subrun_test.go` pins the write-lease behavior on SQLite rather than reasoning about it.
- ~~**What a pre-call refusal looks like to the model**~~ **(answered, W10.1).** Neither of the two this bullet offered. A **synthesized response** — the third option, and the only one that reaches the caller as a report rather than as a broken tool or as silence. Both alternatives the bullet framed were about how much the model can react; the measurement reframed the question as which *field* the reason lands in, and an error lands in the one ADK reserves for a crashed tool. The bullet's real point still stands and now attaches to the answer: a refusal a model can read is a refusal it can loop against, which is the next bullet.
- **Whether the dominant-tool-call detector joins the default set** ([#227](https://github.com/go-steer/mast/issues/227), carried from v0.5, which had an opinion and did not make the call). v0.6 adds a refusal a model can loop against, so this release has a better reason to decide than v0.5 did.
